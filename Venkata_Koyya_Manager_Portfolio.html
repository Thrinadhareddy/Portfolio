03 / Forward-looking philosophy

How I would lead
as a QA Manager

I would focus on giving engineers clarity and autonomy while creating dependable, transparent quality outcomes for delivery stakeholders.

P

People and capability

Build trust through regular coaching, clear expectations, knowledge sharing and growth plans; promote engineering ownership rather than testing as a hand-off.

D

Delivery and partnership

Align testing priorities with product risk, delivery milestones and cross-functional dependencies; surface blockers and decisions early.

Q

Quality and improvement

Use meaningful release signals, defect patterns and stable automation feedback to guide investments and target recurring sources of risk.

04 / First 90 days

A practical entry plan

An initial management roadmap balancing team discovery, delivery stability and visible quality improvements.

DAYS 01–30 · LISTEN

Understand and baseline

  • Meet engineers, managers and key delivery partners.
  • Map release workflow, quality risks and pain points.
  • Baseline pipeline reliability, defect leakage and regression cycle time.
DAYS 31–60 · ALIGN

Agree priorities

  • Set team outcomes, responsibilities and working agreements.
  • Prioritise automation stability and release-governance gaps.
  • Establish a coaching and cross-training rhythm.
DAYS 61–90 · IMPROVE

Demonstrate progress

  • Ship one or two targeted quality improvements.
  • Introduce concise, meaningful quality reporting.
  • Review impact and agree a longer-term improvement plan.
Measures I would establish with the team: regression cycle time · automation stability and failure categorisation · escaped defects · release readiness predictability · team capability development. These are proposed future measures, not historical results.
